# Step Zero as a plain prompt

For ChatGPT, Claude.ai, Gemini or any chat without skills. Paste the whole block as your first message in a new chat, then answer the questions one by one.

```
Before we build anything, do Step 0 with me: the goal, not the feature.

Interview me one question at a time and wait for my answer before the next one. When I describe a solution, ask why and what changes in my day or my business once it exists, until you reach what I actually want.

Cover these in order:
1. Goal: what I want to achieve, how my workday, numbers or business should look after, and how we will know in a month that it worked.
2. Who it is for: who is on the other side, what problems they have around this, what they believe, what they have tried.
3. Where they come from: how this person arrives, what they already know about me and my product at that moment, what mood and how much time they have.
4. What I do not want: what would count as a failure even if it technically works, what is out of scope for now.
5. My concerns: everything I worry about (customer, funnel, texts, structure, tech, price, timing). Collect all of them, then weigh each: how likely, how much damage, what to do about it.
6. Constraints: deadline, budget, stack, people, decisions already made.

For every question offer your own guess as a recommended answer when you can make one from what I already said. Give two to four answer options as a numbered list with your recommended one first, so I can reply with a number or write my own. If I say "not sure", do not move on: research it and bring back a short summary with sources, then ask me to pick. Research means real facts, not guesses: for competitors, five to eight of them with their first-screen promise, prices, free tier and what their users praise and complain about in public reviews; for the audience, what real people wrote in reviews and forums about what they wanted, what they tried and what annoyed them, grouped by similar wants; for the market, size, growth, leaders and what is becoming the minimum users expect, each number with a source and a date.

When you have enough, write concept.md as one document I can copy and save, with these sections: Goal; Who it is for; Where they come from; What we do not want; Concerns, weighed (a table: concern, likelihood, damage, what we do about it); Constraints; Open questions and research; Where the plan is weak (your own view, two to five places where the idea may not reach the goal, and what to check first). Use my words, do not invent facts, put anything you do not know in square brackets as a question. Keep it under two pages.

Show me "Where the plan is weak" and ask me to confirm before anything gets built. From then on, every task starts with reading concept.md.
```

## Grill (roast your idea)

Paste this when you have an idea or a concept.md and want to find what would kill it.

```
Grill my idea. Here it is: [idea or paste concept.md].

Interview me relentlessly, one question at a time, and give your recommended answer to each question. Walk down every branch: who pays and how much, why now, why me, what they do today instead and what it costs them, what breaks at ten times the volume, what has to be true for the numbers to work. Do not accept adjectives as answers: "better", "faster", "simpler" each get a follow-up, better than what, measured how. Check three traps: an empty space in the market that is empty because nobody pays for it (prefer problems people already pay to solve badly); habit and fear of switching, and what my product does about them; a feature mistaken for value, so ask what gets faster, cheaper or less effort for the customer and by how much.

When you have the picture, list the three to five assumptions whose failure kills the idea. For each: the assumption as a checkable sentence, why it is critical, how likely it is wrong and on what evidence, and the cheapest test (five customer conversations, a landing page with a small ad budget, a manual version of the service, a one-day prototype). Order by damage times likelihood and tell me which one to test first.

Plain language, no frameworks by name.
```
